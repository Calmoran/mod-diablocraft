0029 (2026-10-16, `diablocraft-sbxl`, the designer's Life tripwire, card q-1008-14, decisions 2026-10-09) adds `Unit::ExpectedLife(before, max, change)` and `Unit::CheckLifeAfter(source, spell, before, change)`, and asks the second after `Unit::DealDamage` moves a creature's Life (`ModifyHealth(lifeChange)`) and after `Unit::DealHeal` does. The expected Life is the Life before moved by the change and held to [0, max]; a creature whose Life is anywhere else had a 64-bit number cut to 32 bits on the way, and the core writes one `LOG_WARN("entities.unit", "[DC life] creature <entry> <guid>: Life after is <n>, expected <n> (before, change, max); source <guid> spell <id>; seen <n> times")`. Players are not watched (their Life is below 2^32 by the stat code), nothing is sent to the client, and the check is one 64-bit compare per hit or heal. A narrowing hit again and again would fill the log, so the first hundred are written and then one in a thousand, and every line carries its count. It sees a cut in `ModifyHealth`, `SetHealth` and the Life reads; a hit already cut before it reaches `DealDamage` is the census's to find (`test_life64_census.py`). Not on the kill branch: `Unit::Kill` and the creature's AI may legitimately leave any Life. Apply after 0028: `git -C azerothcore-wotlk apply ../core-patches/0029-life-tripwire.patch`. `python core-patches/tests/test_unit_life64.py` compiles `ExpectedLife` and `CheckLifeAfter` from the patched text and shows both halves: silent over every Life and change on both sides of 2^31, 2^32 and 2^63 with the patched `ModifyHealth`, and loud when `curHealth` is cut to 32 bits again; it also kills a mutant for each rule. The line starts `[DC life]`: `grep '\[DC life\]'` over the worldserver log is the check on dev and on live (`dev_log_check.py` reads error lines only).

0028 (2026-10-16, `diablocraft-sbxl`, native 64-bit health Part 1, the hits; plan `docs/proposals/core-64bit-health.md` §2.3 Patch B, changed by the client-half brief) carries a hit in 64 bits along the paths Torment uses. `SpellNonMeleeDamage::damage` and `overkill`, `SpellPeriodicAuraLogInfo::damage` and `overDamage` are `uint64`; the two providers of 0009 and 0016 (`ProvideFinalSpellDamage`, `ProvideFinalPeriodicDamage`) take `uint64& damage` (a module that overrides them changes the parameter, nothing else); `Unit::DealDamageMods` has a `uint64&` form beside the `uint32&` one. The periodic tick keeps the provided tick exact to `DealDamage`: absorb and resist work on a 32-bit view of it (`DamageInfo` is 32-bit) and come off the exact tick by exactly what they took from the view. Every 32-bit consumer inside `DealDamage` (the `UnitScript::DealDamage` hook, the victim's and the attacker's AI callbacks, `OnDamage`, the damage-share auras, rage, Battleground and achievement scores) sees the hit through `ApplyThroughView`: the hit saturated at 4,294,967,295; a consumer that changes its view replaces the hit with what it set, one that leaves it alone leaves the exact hit. One hook is new and nothing mod-ale overrides changes: `UnitScript::ModifyExactDamage(attacker, victim, uint64& damage, damagetype)` is asked first in `DealDamage` with the exact hit, and may lower it (Health Link shares the exact hit there). **What the client-half brief changed from the plan** (point 4): the plan cut every combat log amount at 4,294,967,295 (then 2,147,483,647 for the signed print) and told the module the exact number through an edge hook; there is no clamp and no edge hook now. The spell damage log (`SMSG_SPELLNONMELEEDAMAGELOG`: `Unit::SendSpellNonMeleeDamageLog`, its reflect form, and the gameobject caster's sender in `Object.cpp`, which is the same opcode and so the same layout) and the periodic damage log (`SMSG_PERIODICAURALOG`, aura types 3 and 89) write the hit and the overkill as `uint64`, always; absorbed, resisted and blocked stay 32 bits; the overkill is computed in 64 bits (`LifeOverkill`). Melee (`SMSG_ATTACKERSTATEUPDATE`), the damage shield log, heal logs and party frames stay 32 bits (the plan's list; the client's unsigned fix covers the print). The layouts are `docs/contracts/life-64bit-wire.md`. Known and accepted: stock AI and scripts see a hit of 2^32 or more as 4,294,967,295 (a 'cannot die yet' guard on a boss with more than 4,294,967,295 Life left can miss such a blow; the Guardians run Torment's AI); a flat spell value built from a Life saturates at 2,147,483,647 (decision 7.3). Apply after 0027: `git -C azerothcore-wotlk apply ../core-patches/0028-hits-64bit.patch`. `python core-patches/tests/test_life64_patches.py` pins the shape and the rules above and fails with a patch or a file left out; `python core-patches/tests/test_unit_life64.py` runs `ApplyThroughView`, `LifeOverkill` and the other helpers. The module overrides the two providers, so it does not compile without the signature change: `dc_barbarian_primaries.cpp` and `dc_barbarian_rend.cpp` carry a six-line handover until the Combat cutover (Part 2) removes it. A server with 0027 and 0028 is for the rebuilt client only (a stock client misreads every unit's values update and every damage log): they reach dev together with the client switch, never alone. `test_primary_patch_replay.py` stays red until the PM applies 0027-0029 to the core.

0027 (2026-10-16, `diablocraft-sbxl`, native 64-bit health Part 1, the Life; the designer's rulings q-1008-14 of 2026-10-09 and q-1016-15 of 2026-10-16; plan `docs/proposals/core-64bit-health.md` §2.3 Patch A, census `_agent-scratch/core-64bit-census-2026-10-08/report.md` and `diablocraft-eu9y`) makes a unit's Life and max Life 64 bits. `Unit::m_health64` and `m_maxHealth64` hold them; `GetHealth`, `GetMaxHealth` and the percent helpers return `uint64`; `SetHealth` and `SetMaxHealth` take `uint64`; `ModifyHealth` and `GetHealthGain` take and return `int64` (the pin cast the Life to `int32`, so a creature above 2,147,483,647 dropped to 0, `test_unit_life64.py` runs it); `GetHealthPct` is computed in double. `CountPctFromMaxHealth` and `CountPctFromCurHealth` are whole-number (`CalculateLifePct`, 128-bit through `unsigned __int128`, so the server builds with GCC or Clang, as it does on Linux: `CalculatePct` multiplies in float, which gives 999,999,995,904 for 100% of 1,000,000,000,000). The update fields are the client's copy, written only by `Unit::WriteHealthFields`, and they hold the real Life: the low dword in `UNIT_FIELD_HEALTH` and `UNIT_FIELD_MAXHEALTH`, the high dword in two new fields, `UNIT_FIELD_HEALTH_HI` and `UNIT_FIELD_MAXHEALTH_HI`, that follow `UNIT_FIELD_PADDING` (`UpdateFields.h`, `UpdateFieldFlags.cpp`: the unit block grows from 148 to 150 dwords and every player field moves up by two; a change to either Life dword clears the values-update cache). There is no projection and no 1,000,000,000 bar line: **this is what the client-half brief changed from the plan** (§3.1; brief points 1 and 2, the designer's go of 2026-10-16), and a stock client, refused at login, never meets the layout. The wire is `docs/contracts/life-64bit-wire.md`. `DealDamage` and `DealHeal` take `uint64`; their 32-bit consumers see the amount through `ApplyThroughView` (0028 is the hit's other half); `DealHeal` and `HealBySpell` return `int64`. `Creature`: `_playerDamageReq` and `LowerPlayerDamageReq` are 64-bit, `RegenerateHealth` is 64-bit, `SetBaseHealth(uint64)` holds the exact base and `UpdateMaxHealth` builds the max from it in a `long double` (the `UNIT_MOD_HEALTH` base modifier is a float and cannot hold a Life past 16,777,216), `SelectLevel` calls it; `creature.curhealth` and `character_pet` keep their 32-bit columns and save the Life saturated at 4,294,967,295. The two max-Life auras that keep the health fraction (`HandleAuraModIncreaseMaxHealth`, `HandleAuraModIncreaseHealthPercent`, and the stamina one) keep it in whole numbers. **The narrowing census**: every place a 64-bit Life would be cut without an error was found by making `GetHealth`, `GetMaxHealth` and the two percent helpers return a type that refuses every conversion to a type under 64 bits and building the core, its scripts and the modules: 349 lines in the core (98), its scripts (240), mod-autobalance (7) and mod-ale (4), the same lines as the first census on the pin, and 44 in Torment's module (35 at 41285420); each is a one-line edit by its class (saturate at 4,294,967,295 or, in a spell value, at 2,147,483,647, decision 7.3; widen the variable; drop a cast; or left as it is because a guard keeps it below 2^32), and `core-patches/tests/life64_census.tsv` lists every line with its class (`test_life64_census.py` fails when an edited line comes back or a line leaves the list). What a compiler cannot see, and the review reads: casts through a float or double (`SpellAuraEffects.cpp` max-health aura and `oculus.cpp`, both fixed here, and `Player.cpp` regen, a player), the update fields and the damage structs themselves (this patch and 0028), `src/test` and tools (not built), and runtime paths (SQL, Lua, packets built from another variable: `GroupHandler.cpp` writes a party member's Life through `LifeToUInt32`). AutoBalance (0003's hunks, third-party module): `moduleBaseHealth` and the stored base Life are 64-bit, the refusal above 4,294,967,295 is gone and a module-scaled creature gets `SetBaseHealth`. mod-ale: `unit:GetHealth()` returns ALE's 64-bit object, not a Lua number (no Torment Lua reads Life). Torment's module compiles unchanged; its own narrowing lines (44) are the Combat and World cutovers' (Parts 2 and 3). Apply after 0026 (it edits `Unit.cpp` beside 0002, 0004 and 0022): `git -C azerothcore-wotlk apply ../core-patches/0027-life-64bit.patch`; `modules/mod-autobalance/...` paths start at the core root, like 0019's.
